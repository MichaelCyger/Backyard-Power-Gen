# Notes: what already exists, and what does not

This file is not a homeowner chapter. It is a plain-language map of prior work. Chapters 1 to 7 stay on the house, the yard, and the bill. Chapter 8 is the engineering design basis. This note is only: what the core already is, what other people already built or proposed, and why the Backyard Generating Station is a different object.

The short version: the insides are a known kit. The product is not.

---

## The core is not a new invention

A Backyard Generating Station makes heat by splitting atoms in a sealed fuel, then turns some of that heat into electricity and offers the rest as warmth. That is the same job a small space reactor and a factory-built “nuclear battery” already set out to do.

The specific kit in Chapter 8 is not invented here:

- **TRISO fuel** (tiny coated particles of uranium oxycarbide). Each particle is its own sealed can. This fuel has been made and tested for decades. It is what several current US microreactor programs specify, at the same enrichment class: about 20% U-235 (HALEU, high-assay low-enriched uranium). That is not weapons uranium.
- **A graphite reflector and rotating drums** that hold the reaction or shut it down. NASA’s Kilopower / KRUSTY test used the same idea: drums, not a forest of rods a person has to drive.
- **Heat pipes** (sealed metal tubes that move heat with no pump). Kilopower used them. Westinghouse’s eVinci uses them. The point is the same: nothing in the vault has to circulate coolant with a motor.
- **A Stirling engine** that turns a steady heat flow into electricity. KRUSTY made real watts this way. Commercial wellhead Stirlings already run for years on heat that is not nuclear. Chapter 8 puts that engine on the house side of the chase, because a 100-year sealed Stirling *inside* the vault has not been shown.

So the physics sentence is ordinary: a cold, slow, furnace-scale core (about **35 kW of heat**, about **8 kW of electricity**), TRISO, heat pipes, drums, a paint-can of heavy metal (**about 30 kg / 66 lb at first start**). You could brief that sentence to someone who already works on Kilopower or eVinci and they would recognize the machine.

What has not been shown is that exact kit, sealed, running in a suburban yard for a century. KRUSTY was a short ground test. eVinci is aimed at about **5 MW of electricity** for a mine, a remote town, or a data hall, for on the order of **eight years**, not one house for a hundred. The parts are proven in pieces. The 100-year buried house appliance is not.

## What people already built or proposed

None of the rows below is a single-family product. They are the cousins.

| What | About how big | Who it was for | How close |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| NASA Kilopower / KRUSTY | 1 to 10 kWe | Space: a habitat, a rover, the Moon | Closest *electrical* size. Heat pipes and Stirling. Not a yard. Not a house heat loop. Not a consumer appliance. |
| Westinghouse eVinci | about 5 MWe | Mines, remote towns, industry, military | Same fuel-and-heat-pipe family. About **500 to 1,000 houses**, not one. Target life about 8 years. |
| Westinghouse AstroVinci | 10 kWe to 2 MWe | Satellites and lunar power | Same family scaled down. Space, not a subdivision. |
| Antares R1, Radiant Kaleidos, and similar | about 0.1 to 2 MWe | Remote sites, military, microgrids | Factory boxes. Still village-to-base scale. |
| USNC MMR | a few MWe | Remote sites, industry | Buried on purpose. Still a plant, not a septic-tank vault for one meter. |
| Toshiba 4S (Galena, Alaska) | 10 to 50 MWe | A town far from the grid | Buried deep, sealed, long life on paper. Town-scale. Project died. |
| Hyperion (about 2008) | 25 to 30 MWe | Towns, industry, “20,000 homes” | Sealed “hot tub” module, buried about 10 ft, factory fuel, crane-out. Journalists called it a backyard reactor. It was a small *plant*. Project died. |
| Aalo and other “backyard” headlines | about 10 MWe | Malls, data centers, neighborhoods | The word backyard means “not a 1,000 MW campus.” It does not mean a playset. |
| Research pool reactors (SLOWPOKE, TRIGA) | kilowatts of heat | Campuses and labs | Real kW-class fission. Staffed, opened, not a sealed yard appliance. |
| RTGs (“nuclear batteries” on lighthouses and rovers) | watts | Places you cannot visit | Decay, not a running core. Wrong physics and wrong power. |

Press and essays have also talked about a fridge-sized reactor for a home. Those are not designs. They have no closed numbers, no house piping, no lawn dose, and no approval path.

When a headline says “backyard nuclear,” check the megawatts. If the number has an M in it, it is a neighborhood or a factory, not a house.

## The “hot tub in your backyard” sentence (2007 to now)

The *pitch* is old. Factory-sealed. Buried. Truck in, truck out. Powers homes. No operator on the lot. That paragraph has been in print since about 2007. What they were selling was a **small plant**, not a house appliance.

**Read “hot tub” as the reactor can, not the site.** Hyperion’s module was about **1.5 m across and 2 to 2.5 m tall**, the size of a residential hot tub or two stacked ([Gizmodo, 2007](https://gizmodo.com/hot-tub-nuclear-reactor-could-power-cities-326125); [Fortune, 2010](https://money.cnn.com/2010/03/10/technology/nukes_backyard.fortune/); [Power magazine](https://www.powermag.com/the-race-to-commercialize-mininuclear-reactors/): five-foot-diameter hydride can in a concrete vault about **ten feet underground**). Fortune called the reactor plus protective cabinet “the size of a hot tub” (eight feet by five feet). That is the *object that comes on the truck*. It is not the full footprint. Conversion gear, access, a vault, and a security story still make a plant pad. Hyperion itself said the whole assembly, including the electricity-generating side, sits on **less than an acre**. A Backyard Generating Station asks for about a **10 by 10 foot** patch of grass.

**Read “homes” as a town, not a meter.** Hyperion was **25 to 27 MWe**, sold as power for about **20,000 homes** at about **$25 million** a unit ([Guardian, 9 Nov 2008](https://www.theguardian.com/environment/2008/nov/09/miniature-nuclear-reactors-los-alamos); [Phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2008-11-mini-nuclear-power-homes.html); [Fox](https://www.foxnews.com/story/portable-nuclear-hot-tubs-could-power-america)). That is **about 3,000 times** the electrical size of BGS-8 (8 kWe). ZDNet asked “[A micro nuclear reactor in your garden?](https://www.zdnet.com/article/a-micro-nuclear-reactor-in-your-garden/)” and still meant that 20,000-home box. Fortune titled it “[Nukes in my backyard](https://money.cnn.com/2010/03/10/technology/nukes_backyard.fortune/).” Buried about 15 feet. Swap the can when the core is done. They told the NRC the same logistics in 2010: sealed at the factory, sited underground, returned after seven to ten years ([NRC letter, 25 Feb 2010](https://www.nrc.gov/docs/ML1006/ML100630081.pdf)). Nothing shipped.

**Toshiba said a smaller version of the same sentence.** Rapid-L press in 2007–2008 sold a **200 kWe**, about 20 by 6 foot, 40-year, no-control-rod box for an apartment building, a city block, a basement, or a garage ([Wired, “Toshiba’s Home Nuclear Reactor”](https://www.wired.com/2007/12/toshibas-home-n/); [Engadget, “for your garage?”](https://www.engadget.com/2007-12-19-toshibas-building-a-micro-nuclear-reactor-for-your-garage.html); [Grist](https://grist.org/article/for-reals/)). The Guardian repeated 200 kW / one building next to Hyperion. That was a JAXA/Toshiba space-and-remote concept getting turned into a consumer gadget by blogs. Japan 2008, US/Europe 2009. Did not happen. Even on paper it is **25 times** BGS-8, and it was not a playset-dose lawn with a chase into one house.

**Toshiba 4S / Galena (2004)** used the buried-battery, 30-year, crane-out line for a **town of about 700** at **10 MWe** ([UAF write-up](https://www.uaf.edu/acep-blog/nuclear-power-and-the-perils-of-pioneering.php)). Never built.

**The sentence is still in use.** Aalo’s site still says a trucked **10 MWe** unit is “enough to power **10,000 homes**” ([aalo.com](https://www.aalo.com/)). That is **about 1,250 times** BGS-8. The customer is a data center or a neighborhood, not a single meter.

So: **buried, sealed, no on-site opening, “powers homes”** has been public for about 18 years. What has not been a closed design is **one house, 5 to 10 kWe, septic-tank vault, playset-dose grass, chase into a utility room, compared to rooftop solar at the same meter.** Hyperion used “backyard” and “hot tub” for a 25 MW hole. That is still a plant. The Backyard Generating Station is still a different object. The kit is not novel. The customer and the size class look novel. No one has sold 8 kWe to a homeowner as an appliance.

## What is the same

These are the honest overlaps. Do not overclaim past them.

**The way heat is made.** Splitting atoms in coated particles, at a power density that is low on purpose. A large plant runs hot and hard in each liter of core. This vault, Kilopower, and eVinci all want the opposite: a trickle of heat in a lot of mass, so leftover heat after shutdown is small and the metal ages slowly.

**How heat leaves the core.** Heat pipes. No vault pump you service. That is already the Kilopower and eVinci answer.

**How electricity is made, in the baseline.** A Stirling on a hot end and a cool end. Demonstrated at house-scale watts in a NASA test. Demonstrated as a commercial box on other heat sources. Not demonstrated as a 100-year object welded inside a buried vault. That is why the converter lives at the house.

**Factory sealed, trucked, never opened on site.** Hyperion and Toshiba 4S already sold that sentence: fuel it at the plant, bury it, leave it, crane it out, take it back. The Backyard Generating Station uses the same logistics idea. It applies it to a vault the size of a large septic tank, not a 10 to 30 MW hole.

**Bury it for people and weather.** 4S, Hyperion, and MMR all put the core underground. Dirt as extra shield and as a place the neighbors do not see a building. Chapter 3 still designs the vault to meet the lawn dose with the lid bare. Soil is bonus. That is a tighter claim than “we buried it, so it is fine.”

**Combined heat and power.** Any of those cores makes heat and electricity together. Mines and towns already talk about process heat and district heat. The difference is whose pipes: a campus loop versus a house floor, a tank, or a greenhouse.

## What is different

The customer is one house. The comparison is rooftop solar at the same meter. That is the whole product.

**Size.** Industry “microreactors” start around **100 kWe** and live at **1 to 5 MWe**. The Backyard Generating Station is **5 to 10 kWe** (type unit **BGS-8: 8 kWe / 35 kWth**). That is the same neighborhood as an 8 to 12 kW solar array, as a steady output, sun or not. Kilopower is the only well-known machine in that electrical band, and it was not for a kitchen.

**Who owns it and who operates it.** The others are sold to a utility, a mine, the military, or a town. Staff or a remote control room still exist in the story. Here the homeowner owns an appliance. They do not hold the startup locks. They do not become a plant operator. Chapter 6 is that bet.

**How long it stays closed.** eVinci-class targets are about 8 years. Hyperion was 5 to 7. 4S claimed 10 to 30. The vault is specified for on the order of **100 years** without opening. That life is a design aim, not a test that has already been run. The honest sentence is: the fuel inventory and the low power density make a century *thinkable*. A century in the ground has not been demonstrated.

**Two products at the house.** Electricity into a wall battery and an inverter the solar trade already knows. Warmth into hydronic floors, a tank, a hydro-air coil, a boiler loop, a greenhouse, or a small dry cooler in summer. The others treat heat as a campus or process load. This one treats heat as a house load. Summer dump is a design job, not a free extra.

**The lawn, not the fence.** The dose target is **≤5 mrem/year** if someone lived on the grass 24 hours a day (about one cross-country flight). A playset at an hour a day is a small slice of that. A plant site argues a perimeter. This product argues a playset.

**How the pipes leave.** Floor only, then a bent chase, so a shine looks at dirt. That is a house-and-yard detail the mine boxes do not have to get right.

**The approval ask.** The others still want to be licensed as small plants. The Backyard Generating Station’s bet is a **size-based appliance path**: one factory design, many lots, no custom 1,000 MW stamp behind each house. That stamp does not exist yet. Nobody has asked for it at 8 kWe, because nobody has been selling 8 kWe to a homeowner.

**The end of the story.** Crane-out, recycle the box, leftover heavy metal in a paint-can receptacle at a licensed site, swap price includes leaving. Hyperion and 4S had a take-back sentence. They did not have a consumer swap next to a solar-plus-battery quote.

**What it is compared to.** Those machines are compared to diesel, to a transmission line, or to a 1,000 MW campus. This one is compared to a complete rooftop solar-plus-battery job at the same address: what you pay, what you maintain, what you get, what you can sell back.

## What not to say

Do not say the Backyard Generating Station invented TRISO, heat pipes, or Stirling conversion. It did not.

Do not say a 100-year sealed yard life is already proven. It is not. The pieces are. The century is a specification.

Do not say “backyard reactor” as if Hyperion or Aalo already did this. They used the word for a 10 to 25 MWe plant. “Hot tub” was the reactor can, not the site. See the press section above.

Do not say a fridge-sized home reactor is already a product. It is a video.

Do not say the core is unproven in the same breath as “nobody has done house scale.” Both can be true: the *kit* is known, the *appliance* is not.

---

## One sentence for a lawyer or an investor

The core is Kilopower- and eVinci-class physics (TRISO, HALEU, heat pipes, drums, Stirling on the house side) pointed at one meter. The novelty, if any, is the product: a factory-sealed, septic-tank-sized vault, 5 to 10 kWe, heat and electricity into a house, playset-dose lawn, 100-year lock, crane-out swap, and a size-based appliance ask. Prior art is full of the kit. It is empty of that customer.

---

## Marketing ideas

Do not invent a smaller reactor class (picoreactor, millireactor). That still says plant. The class word is **appliance**. Category under the name: **home generating appliance**.

If someone asks whether it is an SMR: no. That is a small plant. This is a house appliance. Same job as rooftop solar, buried in the yard.

One-liners that sell:

- **One house. One appliance. Power and heat, day and night.**
- **Set it like a tank. Use it like solar.**

The title page already has a close version of the first: “One house. One backyard generating station. Electricity and heat, day and night, for 100 years.” Keep the product name on the book. Use “appliance” when you are selling the class.
