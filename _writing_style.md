# Writing style

Use this file for every Backyard Generating Station chapter.

Chapter order: 1 The Need, 2 The Idea, 3 Yard Safety, 4 The House, 5 The Vault, 6 The Approval, 7 The Swap. Engineering design basis: 8 Vault Spec.

## Voice

- High level, then a simple number when the physics has to land.
- Talk to a smart homeowner, not a regulator and not a child.
- Factual. No jokes, no wink lines, no “the yard is not a tank farm.”
- Specific over slang. Do not write “septic money.” Write what the job is and what it costs.
- Generic US house, not one waterfront lot. A high-use bill can be the top of a range. Do not narrate a personal quote as if it were the national case.
- Flow top to bottom. One idea per section. Later chapters can go deeper; this pass has to make sense alone.
- Convince by being exact, not by stacking adjectives.

## Product name

- Full name: **Backyard Generating Station**.
- In a table or a contrast with a 1,000 MW plant, write the full name. Do not say “Station” alone there.
- The buried object is **the vault**. Never “capsule.” Vault sounds sealed and safe. “The core” is what is inside it.

## Nuclear

- Do not lead with nuclear. Lead with the house, the yard, the bill, or the object in the ground.
- Name fission only in the section that explains how power is made, or in a later chapter that cannot be honest without it (shielding, fuel, end of life).
- Even then, keep the scale: furnace heat, paint-can of fuel (~30 kg / 66 lb loaded), septic-tank vault.

## Units and numbers

- First time a unit appears, say what it is in parentheses, with a house-scale example.
- kW is how hard power is flowing right now. kWh is how much you used over time. kWe is kilowatts of electricity, not heat.
- mrem is a year’s-dose kind of unit. Compare it to ordinary background, not to a plant accident.
- Give kilograms and pounds for US readers.
- Ranges over fake precision. Say what the range depends on (rate, sun, sell-back, dry soil).
- Simple arithmetic in the open when payback or dose depends on it.
- Do not add a References section at the end. When a chapter is documenting the public need, link primary sources inline (EIA, HHS, a major paper). Do not cite in other chapters unless the user asks.

## Punctuation and sentences

- Never use an em dash unless it is clearly better than a period, a comma, a colon, parentheses, or a recast. Default to those. We have used far too many.
- Prefer “to” in numeric ranges (10,000 to 12,000) over en dashes.
- Short paragraphs. Complete sentences. No filler openers (“It is important to note”).

## What not to do

- No Home / Away / Storm operating modes. An app is a status window, not a control room.
- No hair-dryer-to-house comparisons. Keep like-for-like: house, rooftop solar, Backyard Generating Station, commercial plant.
- No “only a fraction becomes electricity.” Heat and electricity are two products.
- No panel-only solar stickers. Compare a complete solar-plus-battery job at the same house.
- No placeholders, “TBD,” or jokes in Benefits / Detriments.
