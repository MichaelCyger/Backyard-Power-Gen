# The Swap

Nothing is opened in the yard. Replacement means the old vault leaves as one piece and a new one sits in the same hole. The price of that leaving is part of replacement, not a chore the household discovers in year ninety-nine.

---

## The day it comes out

A licensed crew isolates the house: gateway open, heat loop stopped, inverter off. They put the **shutdown locks back in** the same way Chapter 5 put them in at the factory. The vault stops making heat on purpose. Leftover heat is already small. They wait the hours it takes for that to fall further if the procedure says to wait.

Then a crane lifts the vault, still sealed, onto a permitted truck. The hole that is left is a known shape. The next vault, locked and cold, goes in. Chapter 4’s chase lands again. Inspectors sign. A licensed startup is the first heat from the new unit.

The homeowner does not store, cut, or dispose of anything. There is no pile of old parts by the garage. There is no “we’ll get the core later.”

If a unit is swapped early (damage, a recall, a sale that wants a newer vault), the day looks the same. Early or late, it is a crane event.

---

## What happens to the old vault

The truck goes to a licensed facility, not to the town dump.

**The box.** Lead, steel, and concrete are recycled or reused where that is possible. The vault was almost all shield and box. That mass is ordinary industrial material once it is clean enough for the facility’s rules.

**The fuel.** The work began as a coffee-can of heavy metal: on the order of **10 kilograms (about 20 pounds)**. After a century, what cannot be recycled or reused is still in that size class. It goes into a coffee-can receptacle at a licensed site. It does not become a hillside behind the house. It does not stay in the hole.

The owner never takes title to a waste drum. Take-back is the factory’s (or the licensed operator’s) job, paid as part of the swap.

---

## What the check includes

A replacement is sold as a **swap**:

- new vault
- removal of the old one
- haul
- processing (recycle what you can, receptacle for what you cannot)
- reconnect of power and heat
- licensed shutdown and licensed startup

It should not be a surprise line item after someone has lived with the appliance for decades.

How that is paid can be any of these, or a mix:

- **Prepaid at first install.** A take-back fee sits in the original price, like a deposit on a tank.
- **Included core-exchange.** The company that sold the vault is on the hook to swap it. The homeowner pays a posted swap price that already assumes take-back.
- **A posted tariff at the time of swap.** Clear dollars, known in advance as a class of job, not a custom demolition bid.

Which of those wins is a business detail. The rule is the same: **replacement means the old station leaves, and the cost of that leaving is inside the replacement.**

The hole, the crane path, and the chase were supposed to stay usable (Chapter 4). If a later owner built a garage on the lift path, that is their extra bill, not a reason to cut the vault open in place.

---

## What is not the swap

**Batteries and the inverter** die on a much shorter clock, often 10 to 15 years. Replacing those is a wall job, like solar. It is not a crane day and not Chapter 7.

**The outdoor radiator or a house pump** is a hardware-store-class failure. Not a vault event.

**A playset or a patio** on the soil is fine if footings stay shallow (Chapter 3). Deep posts into the lid are how a swap becomes a fight.

**Stacking old solar panels on a pallet** is the wrong picture. The leftover fuel is small. The process is specialized. Both things are true at once.

---

## A century, or sooner

The vault is designed to run for on the order of a hundred years without a hatch. That is a design life, not a promise that the first owner’s great-grandchildren must keep the same serial number. A swap can happen when the unit is done, when a better vault exists, or when the house is reworked.

Until that day, there is no refueling truck and no backyard cracking. After that day, the story is one lift, one new vault, one coffee-can at a licensed site.

---

## What this document is

This is the end: locks in, crane out, same hole, recycle the box, coffee-can the rest, and a replacement price that already includes leaving. Chapters 3 to 6 are how the yard, the house, the vault, and the stamp make that day boring. The rule here is: if the household is asked to keep a piece, the swap failed.
